Games are one of the most natural ways for children to hear and speak English. The rules create a reason to communicate, and the excitement means children repeat words and phrases many times without getting bored. The ten games below need no phone or tablet – just a few things you already have at home, and a little enthusiasm from the grown-ups.

## Choosing the right game

Before you start, keep three things in mind:

- **Age and attention span.** Younger children do well with active games with simple rules; older children enjoy games with a challenge or a puzzle to solve.
- **Language goal.** Each game should focus on one group of words or one sentence pattern.
- **Length.** 5–15 minutes is plenty. Stop while your child is still enjoying it.

## 1. Simon says

**Suitable for:** ages 3 and up. **Practises:** verbs, body parts, listening.

The leader gives a command: “Simon says touch your nose!” – and everyone follows. If a command doesn’t start with “Simon says” and someone still follows it, they’re out. Once your child is used to the game, let them be the leader.

:::phrases
- Simon says jump.
- Simon says clap your hands.
- Touch your ears!
:::

## 2. I spy

**Suitable for:** ages 4 and up. **Practises:** colours, shapes, initial letters.

One person says “I spy with my little eye something… blue!” and the other guesses the object. For older children, switch the clue to the first letter: “something beginning with B”.

## 3. What’s in the bag?

**Suitable for:** ages 3 and up. **Practises:** names of objects, describing adjectives.

Put a few objects in a cloth bag. Your child reaches in, feels an object, describes it and guesses: “It’s soft. It’s small. Is it a ball?” The game encourages children to use adjectives and ask questions.

## 4. Treasure hunt

**Suitable for:** ages 4 and up. **Practises:** prepositions of place, names of objects.

Hide 5–6 objects around the room. Give clues in English: “It’s under the table.” “It’s behind the door.” For children who can read, write the clues on paper.

:::phrases
- in / on / under
- behind / next to
- Is it in the box?
:::

## 5. What’s missing?

**Suitable for:** ages 4 and up. **Practises:** remembering vocabulary, names of objects.

Lay out 5–6 objects or picture cards on the table and name them together. Your child closes their eyes while you take one away. Your child opens their eyes and guesses: “The spoon is missing!”

## 6. Freeze dance

**Suitable for:** ages 3 and up. **Practises:** listening, movement verbs.

Put on a song and the whole family dances. When the music stops, the leader calls out an action: “Freeze!”, “Sit down!”, “Hop like a bunny!”. The game gets children moving and responding to commands in English.

## 7. Shop role-play

**Suitable for:** ages 4 and up. **Practises:** polite everyday phrases, counting.

Use fruit and toys as the goods and paper as money. One person is the shopkeeper, the other the customer. Swap roles after each turn.

:::phrases
- Can I have two apples, please?
- Here you are.
- How much is it?
- Thank you! Goodbye!
:::

## 8. Twenty questions

**Suitable for:** ages 7 and up. **Practises:** yes/no questions, topic vocabulary.

One person thinks of an animal. The other asks up to 20 yes/no questions to guess it: “Can it fly?” “Is it big?” “Does it live in the water?” You can narrow the topic (animals, food, jobs) so the game matches your child’s vocabulary.

## 9. Story chain

**Suitable for:** ages 7 and up. **Practises:** making sentences, creativity, storytelling.

The first person begins: “Once upon a time, there was a cat.” The next person adds a sentence. The story can get very silly – and that’s exactly what makes children want to keep playing. Your child can slip in words from their home language when they don’t know the English yet; you then suggest the matching English word.

## 10. Charades

**Suitable for:** ages 5 and up. **Practises:** verbs, animals, jobs.

One person acts something out without speaking. Everyone else guesses in English: “You’re swimming!” “Are you a doctor?”

## Making the games really work

- **Grown-ups play for real.** Children are far more excited when parents join in properly – playing the part, losing and laughing too.
- **Repeat familiar games.** Children love playing the same game again and again; every replay is a chance to revise.
- **Increase the challenge gradually.** Add new words and new rules once your child has mastered the game.
- **Praise the effort.** Don’t turn the game into a test.

## Conclusion

Games help English become a pleasure rather than a chore. With just a few things from around the house and 10 minutes a day, you can create memorable language experiences for your child.
