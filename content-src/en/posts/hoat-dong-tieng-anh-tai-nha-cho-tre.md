Many parents would like their children to hear more English but don’t know where to begin, or assume they need to set aside special “study time”. In fact, the familiar things you do every day – breakfast, bath time, hanging out the washing, going to the market – are already full of chances for your child to hear and use English. Below are 15 activities organised by time of day, each with ready-to-use phrases so you can try them straight away.

:::callout
**How to use this article:** There’s no need to do everything at once. Pick 2–3 activities that fit your family’s routine, keep them going for 1–2 weeks, then add a new one. The phrases are deliberately short, so they’re easy to use even for parents who are just starting to learn English themselves.
:::

## In the morning

### 1. Good morning feelings

Each morning, ask your child one question about how they feel and hint at the answer with your facial expression.

:::phrases
- Good morning!
- How are you today?
- I’m happy / sleepy / hungry.
:::

After a few days, swap roles: let your child ask you.

### 2. Getting dressed on “command”

Turn getting dressed into a follow-the-instructions game. Speak slowly and point to each item of clothing.

:::phrases
- Put on your shirt.
- Now your socks!
- Left foot. Right foot.
:::

### 3. Counting and naming at breakfast

Count the pieces of toast or the grapes on the plate; name the foods and ask which one your child likes.

:::phrases
- How many grapes?
- One, two, three!
- Do you like eggs?
:::

## Around the house

### 4. Label everyday objects

Write English names on sticky notes and put them on familiar objects: door, window, fridge, sofa, lamp. Each time someone walks past, the whole family touches the label and reads it aloud. After a week, take the labels off and quiz your child.

### 5. Sorting clothes by colour

When folding or hanging out the washing, ask your child to sort the clothes by colour and say the colours in English.

:::phrases
- What colour is this?
- It’s blue.
- Put the red socks here.
:::

### 6. Tidying up as a “mission”

Instead of saying “tidy your toys”, give your child a specific mission in English. Children are often far keener when tidying up becomes a game.

:::phrases
- Let’s tidy up!
- Put the cars in the box.
- Where’s the teddy bear?
:::

### 7. Float or sink at bath time

Drop a few objects into a tub of water and guess which ones will float and which will sink. This activity combines simple science exploration with language.

:::phrases
- Will it float or sink?
- It floats!
- It sinks!
:::

Always have an adult beside your child during water play.

## In the kitchen

### 8. Little kitchen helper

Give your child safe jobs such as washing vegetables, counting eggs or stirring batter. Each job comes with a verb: wash, count, mix, pour.

:::phrases
- Can you wash the tomatoes?
- Mix, mix, mix!
- Pour the water, please.
:::

### 9. Tasting and describing flavours

Let your child taste different flavours and describe them with simple adjectives.

:::phrases
- It’s sweet.
- It’s sour!
- It’s salty.
- Yummy! / Yuck!
:::

Remember to check for any food allergy risks before letting your child taste things.

## Out and about

### 10. Market and supermarket trips with a picture list

Draw or print pictures of 4–5 things you need to buy and write the English name next to each one. At the supermarket, your child is the “product finder”.

:::phrases
- We need bananas.
- Can you find the milk?
- I found it!
:::

### 11. “I spy…” on the go

In the car or on foot, play a game of spotting things by colour or shape.

:::phrases
- I spy something red.
- Is it a car?
- Yes! / No, try again.
:::

### 12. Watching the weather

Every day, look at the sky with your child and talk about the weather. You could draw a small weather chart to stick on the fridge.

:::phrases
- What’s the weather like?
- It’s sunny / rainy / cloudy / windy.
:::

## In the evening

### 13. Sharing one happy moment from the day

Before bed, everyone shares the thing they liked best about their day. Your child can mix their home language with English; you then repeat the sentence back in simple English.

:::phrases
- What did you do today?
- I played with my friend.
- I liked the park.
:::

### 14. Reading bilingual or wordless picture books

With a wordless picture book, you and your child name what you see in the pictures together: “Look, a cat! The cat is sleeping.” What matters is chatting together about the pictures, not reading every word correctly.

### 15. The same goodnight words every night

A goodnight phrase repeated every evening helps English become a warm family habit.

:::phrases
- Good night!
- Sweet dreams!
- I love you.
:::

## Tips for keeping it going

- **Repetition is key.** Use the same phrase for several days before adding a new one.
- **Don’t correct too much.** When your child makes a mistake, simply repeat the correct sentence naturally instead of saying “that’s wrong”. For example, if your child says “Me want milk”, you reply: “Oh, you want milk? Here you are!”
- **Celebrate small progress.** Note down the words your child says on their own in a notebook – it’s motivating for both of you.
- **Imperfect pronunciation is fine.** You can listen to pronunciations in an online dictionary or to English songs with your child, then practise together.

## Conclusion

English doesn’t have to wait for lesson time. When it is woven into familiar routines, the language becomes more meaningful and easier for children to remember. Start small, be patient and enjoy these moments with your child.
