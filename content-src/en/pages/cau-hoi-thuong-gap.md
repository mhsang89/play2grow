## About Play2Grow

:::details What is Play2Grow?
Play2Grow is a centre for children offering STEAM English plus art (drawing, arts and crafts), music (piano, keyboard) and dance (TikTok Dance, modern dance) classes. We focus on helping children learn through hands-on activities, play, exploration and experiences connected to everyday life. Our spirit in one line: *Explore the world. Play with English. Grow every day.*
:::

## About our approach

:::details How is learning through experience different from traditional learning?
Instead of starting with word lists or grammar rules, experiential learning starts with a real activity – a game, a discovery session, an everyday task. Language is introduced and used right inside that activity. See [Our approach]({{page:phuong-phap-hoc}}) for details.
:::

:::details Does my child need to know English already?
Not necessarily. Experiential activities can start from very simple words and phrases. What matters is that the activity fits the child’s age and current level.
:::

:::details Will learning through experience help my child do better in exams?
Play2Grow does not promise exam results or scores. Learning through experience aims to help children enjoy English and use it with confidence; to prepare for an exam, children still need structured practice for that exam’s requirements.
:::

:::details How long before I see progress?
There is no single answer for every child. Progress depends on age, how much English the child hears and uses, consistency and interest. Rather than comparing with other children, look for small changes: a new phrase understood, a word used spontaneously, an activity your child gets excited about.
:::

## Supporting your child at home

:::details My English isn’t good. Can I still support my child?
Absolutely. Parents don’t need to act as teachers; the important role is creating opportunities, encouraging and learning together. The article [Can parents who aren’t fluent in English help their children learn?]({{post:cha-me-khong-gioi-tieng-anh-giup-con-hoc}}) has practical ideas.
:::

:::details How much time should we spend each day?
For young children, 10–15 happy minutes a day is usually easier to keep up than one long session. Stop while your child is still enjoying it, so they want to continue next time.
:::
