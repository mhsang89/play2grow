"Approach – How English enters a child’s day"
"Look at a leaf, an ant or a cloud, then name and describe it in English. Curiosity is a natural reason for children to want one more new word."
"Play"
"Games create reasons to speak: whose turn it is, what we found, who got there first. Children repeat words many times without it feeling like an exercise."
"Everyday life"
"Breakfast, getting dressed, tidying toys… everyday routines are chances to use the same phrases again and again, naturally."
"Communication"
"Asking and answering, retelling, playing shopkeeper and customer. Children use English to get something done, not just to answer a question in a lesson."
"Creativity"
"Drawing, cutting and sticking, building models and then presenting their work. When children make something themselves, they have a lot to say about it."
"Growing every day"
"Language needs time to grow. Small, regular, happy experiences are worth more than long, stressful lessons."

"Philosophy – Passive learning vs experiential learning"
"The Play2Grow philosophy"
"English feels closer when children have a reason to use it"
"Memorising vocabulary and sentence patterns is a necessary part of learning a language, but it is not enough. A new word only truly becomes “theirs” when a child hears it in a meaningful situation, tries saying it, gets a response and uses it again many times. That is why Play2Grow starts from experience."
"When English stays on the page"
"Memorising lists of separate words, rarely linked to real things"
"Most of the time is spent listening and copying"
"Few chances to speak in a real situation"
"English is separate from the child’s daily life"
"When English becomes part of experience"
"Words are linked to real objects, actions and feelings"
"Children observe, ask questions, try and speak for themselves"
"Mistakes are seen as a natural part of learning"
"English appears at mealtimes, playtime and on walks"
"This is not an “either/or” choice. Structured learning still matters; experiential learning gives children more chances to use what they have learnt."
